# Domain 1: Information Security Governance (18%)

## A. Enterprise governance

### Organizational culture

- **Governance** sets direction and oversight (the board and senior management). **Management** plans and carries it out.
- **Information security governance** is part of corporate governance. It makes sure security supports business objectives, manages risk and uses resources responsibly.
- **Outcomes of good security governance:**
  - **strategic alignment** with business goals
  - **risk management**
  - **value delivery**
  - **resource management**
  - **performance measurement**
  - **assurance process integration**
- **Culture:** a positive security culture comes from leadership "tone at the top" and treats security as everyone's responsibility.
- **Risk appetite and risk tolerance** are set by senior management and the board.

### Legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements

- Identify all requirements that apply: privacy laws (GDPR, CCPA), industry rules (PCI DSS, HIPAA, SOX), contracts and SLAs.
- **Due care and due diligence:** reasonable protection and ongoing verification.
- Compliance is a **minimum**, not the goal. Security should be **risk-based** and aligned to the business.

### Organizational structures, roles, and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| **Board of directors** | Ultimate accountability; oversight; sets risk appetite |
| **Executive management / steering committee** | Sets strategy, approves policy, provides resources |
| **CISO / information security manager** | Develops and manages the security program; advises management |
| **Business process / data owners** | Accountable for their information assets; classify data and accept risk |
| **Data custodians** | Implement controls day to day |
| **Information security steering committee** | Cross-functional group that aligns security with business priorities |
| **Internal audit** | Independent assurance |

- **RACI chart:** responsible, accountable, consulted, informed.
- The **information security manager advises**; **business owners and senior management make risk decisions.**

> **Exam tip:** CISM asks you to think like an **information security manager advising the business.** The best answer usually aligns with business objectives, involves senior management or risk owners, and doesn't jump straight into technical fixes.

## B. Information security strategy

### Information security strategy development

- **Strategy** connects the **current state** to the **desired state**, closing the gap with a **roadmap.**
- **Steps:**
  1. Understand business goals and risk appetite.
  2. Assess the current state (gap analysis, maturity assessment).
  3. Define the desired state.
  4. Build the roadmap and business case.
  5. Get senior management approval and resources.
- **Inputs:** business strategy, risk assessments, BIA results, audit findings, regulatory requirements, threat landscape.
- **Constraints:** budget, legal requirements, culture, staff skills, time, technology.
- **Business case:** justifies investment in business terms (value, cost, risk reduction, ROI).
- **Balanced scorecard** and **maturity models** (e.g., CMMI) to show progress.

### Enterprise architecture (new in the 2026 outline)

- **Enterprise architecture (EA):** the structure of an organization's business processes, information, applications and technology.
- **Security's role:** make sure security is built into the architecture and aligns with business goals.
- **Frameworks:**
  - **TOGAF:** enterprise architecture framework and development method.
  - **Zachman Framework:** a matrix of what, how, where, who, when and why across perspectives.
  - **SABSA:** **security** architecture framework; business-driven and risk-based.

### Information governance frameworks and standards

| Framework / standard | Purpose |
|----------------------|---------|
| **COBIT 2019** | Governance and management of enterprise IT (ISACA) |
| **ISO/IEC 27001** | Requirements for an ISMS (certifiable) |
| **ISO/IEC 27002** | Control guidance |
| **NIST CSF 2.0** | Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover |
| **NIST SP 800-53** | Security and privacy control catalog |
| **ITIL** | IT service management |

- **Policy hierarchy:** policies (management intent) → standards (mandatory specifics) → procedures (step by step) → guidelines (recommendations).
- **Metrics:** KGIs (key goal indicators), KPIs (key performance indicators), KRIs (key risk indicators). Good metrics are meaningful to the audience, accurate, timely and cost-effective.

> **Exam tip:** The **first step** in developing a security strategy is understanding **business objectives**, not picking controls or frameworks.
